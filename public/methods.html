Methods & sources

Amsterdam is calculated and ranked by official neighbourhood. The three indicators remain surface temperature, green deficit and low income. The four weight sets are unchanged. No Amsterdam 500 m grid or transferred cell income remains.

Coverage

518 official buurten valid on 24 March 2022; 418 published O&S income observations; 411 complete neighbourhoods ranked. 100 have no income record in the complete source response; another 7 have published income but no observed surface temperature. Missing values remain null; grey means unranked.

Income

O&S IHHINK_MED, median disposable household income in euros, joined directly by buurt code. Student households and institutional residents are excluded. This is not standardised for household size, is different from the previous CBS standardised-income series, and is not a poverty rate. Household composition can affect neighbourhood medians.

The API period label is 2024, with Peildatum 1 January 2024; the underlying tax-income reference year has not been independently verified. The indicator definition limits publication to areas with at least 100 households. An absent record does not establish the specific reason for suppression in each area. Metadata mentions rounding to hundreds, while returned numbers are not all multiples of 100; values are retained unchanged.

Surface temperature

Direct polygon extraction from the existing Landsat 8/9 JJA 2019–2023 composite, native 30 m EPSG:3035. Each neighbourhood receives the median of finite summer_lst_median_c pixels with summer_lst_valid_count > 0 on non-water land. At least 50% of its land pixels must have observed temperature. This is land-surface temperature, not air temperature. Seven income-published neighbourhoods have no observations in the composite.

The supplied project raster permits reproduction of neighbourhood extraction. The original scene-selection and cloud-mask script is unavailable; full reconstruction of the satellite composite is not claimed.

Green deficit

ESA WorldCover 2021 v200, native approximately 10 m: tree cover (class 10) plus grassland (30), divided by observed non-water area. Deficit is 100 minus that share. Water (80) is excluded from the denominator; water percentage alone does not exclude a neighbourhood. Cropland and shrubland are not included in this deliberately narrow green indicator. It is not a full model of cooling capacity.

Pixels are included by their centre inside the official polygon, not by fractional boundary intersection. WorldCover proportions use cos(latitude) row weights for geographic pixel areas. The WorldCover land mask is resampled by nearest neighbour to the thermal raster. Small or narrow areas may be sensitive to pixel-centre inclusion. No old grid score is averaged into the neighbourhood result.

Scores and weightings

All three indicators use the same 411 complete neighbourhoods as the percentile reference. Average-rank percentile = 100 × (rank − 1)/(N − 1), with ties equal; income is reversed. Higher heat, greater green deficit and lower income give higher scores.

Weighted sums are calculated after neighbourhood normalization. The scenario median is the median of four sums, not public agreement. All scenarios share the same complete observations. Priority Lines and Rankings read the same scores and polygons.

Eligibility requires published income, at least 99% observed land cover and at least 50% observed land-temperature coverage. These thresholds are analyst choices, not provider rules. Area coverage does not measure household coverage. Raw observations remain downloadable for unranked areas; percentile and composite scores are null.

Brussels and limitations

Brussels boundaries and observations are preserved; no ranking is published until comparable neighbourhood income is available. Cities are not ranked against each other. Input dates differ. Neighbourhood medians hide within-area inequalities. No predictive validation or new spatial-scale sensitivity metric is claimed.
