Methods & sources

Both cities are calculated and ranked separately by official neighbourhood, using surface temperature, green deficit and lower median income. Brussels refers to the Brussels-Capital Region, not the City of Brussels municipality. Lower median income gives a higher income score; it is not the percentage of low-income residents. No 500 m grid scores enter either model.

Calculation order

  1. Read the dated source data and official boundaries.
  2. Align rasters with the neighbourhood polygons.
  3. Extract neighbourhood temperature; join Amsterdam public-green share and income by code; extract Brussels raster green cover.
  4. Check missing data and coverage; retain complete neighbourhoods for scoring.
  5. Calculate within-city percentiles and reverse the income direction.
  6. Combine scores using the four weight sets; calculate their scenario median.
  7. Verify joins, score direction, ties and weighted formulas.
  8. Interpret the results alongside documents and interviews.

Grey means unranked, not low priority. The prototype tests weighting changes while holding indicators and boundaries fixed. It does not establish democratic legitimacy or test alternative spatial scales.

Amsterdam: coverage

110 official wijken valid on 24 March 2022; 108 published O&S income observations; 108 complete neighbourhoods ranked. 2 wijken have no income record in the complete source response. All 108 income-published wijken meet the physical-indicator coverage rules. Missing values remain null; grey means unranked.

Amsterdam: income definition

O&S IHHINK_MED, median disposable household income in euros, joined directly by wijk code. Student households and institutional residents are excluded. This is not standardised for household size, is different from the previous CBS standardised-income series, and is not a poverty rate. Household composition can affect neighbourhood medians.

The API period label is 2024, with Peildatum 1 January 2024; the underlying tax-income reference year has not been independently verified. The indicator definition limits publication to areas with at least 100 households. An absent record does not establish the specific reason for suppression in each area. Metadata mentions rounding to hundreds, while returned numbers are not all multiples of 100; values are retained unchanged.

Surface temperature

Direct polygon extraction from the existing Landsat 8/9 JJA 2019–2023 composite, native 30 m EPSG:3035. Each neighbourhood receives the median of finite summer_lst_median_c pixels with summer_lst_valid_count > 0 on non-water land. At least 50% of its land pixels must have observed temperature. This is land-surface temperature, not air temperature. All 108 income-published Amsterdam wijken meet the observed-temperature coverage rule.

The supplied project raster permits reproduction of neighbourhood extraction. The original scene-selection and cloud-mask script is unavailable; full reconstruction of the satellite composite is not claimed.

Green deficit

Amsterdam: O&S public-green inventory, snapshot 1 January 2025, geography 24 March 2022. ORPUBGROEN_P = public green hectares / total land hectares. Source fractions are multiplied by 100; green deficit = 100 minus public-green percentage. Joined directly by official wijk code; all 110 wijken have values. Private gardens and green roofs are not added. This measures area, not accessibility, ownership rights or cooling performance. Source and metadata.

Brussels: official Bruxelles Environnement WFS public_green_spaces, retrieved 26 October 2026; the inventory reference year has not been verified. Select 1,204 polygons whose status_foncier is Public and type is Espace vert or Espace public végétalisé. Private and mixed ownership are excluded. Repair geometries, union polygons to avoid overlaps, intersect with each neighbourhood in EPSG:31370, divide by total neighbourhood polygon area. This is green-space area, including paths and other surfaces inside each site, not pure vegetated cover. Two unclassified inventory features affect Kriekenput - Homborch - Verrewinkel and Saint-Job Kauwberg: their green values and rankings remain null, not zero.

Denominator limit: Amsterdam uses provider land area excluding water; Brussels uses full polygon area including water. Definitions are not identical and shares cannot be compared directly across cities. Both are separate within-city experiments.

For the temperature land mask only, pixels are included by their centre inside the official polygon, not by fractional boundary intersection. WorldCover proportions use cos(latitude) row weights for geographic pixel areas. The WorldCover land mask is resampled by nearest neighbour to the thermal raster. Small or narrow areas may be sensitive to pixel-centre inclusion. No old grid score is averaged into the neighbourhood result.

Scores and weightings

All three indicators use the same complete neighbourhoods within each city as the percentile reference: 108 in Amsterdam and 116 in Brussels. Each city has its own reference distribution. Average-rank percentile = 100 × (rank − 1)/(N − 1), with ties equal; income is reversed. Higher heat, greater green deficit and lower median income give higher scores. A neighbourhood median does not describe every household or measure the proportion of residents below a poverty threshold.

Weighted sums are calculated after neighbourhood normalization. The scenario median is the median of four sums, not public agreement. All scenarios share the same complete observations. Priority Lines and Rankings read the same scores and polygons.

Eligibility requires published income, at least 99% observed land cover and at least 50% observed land-temperature coverage. These thresholds are analyst choices, not provider rules. Area coverage does not measure household coverage. Raw observations remain downloadable for unranked areas; percentile and composite scores are null.

Brussels-Capital Region: coverage and income

Brussels now uses IBSA/Statbel indicator 2498: median equivalised after-tax income of residents, 2023. Of 145 official Monitoring des Quartiers areas, 118 have a published income value; 116 can be ranked after excluding two neighbourhoods with unresolved green-inventory features. The 27 dash-marked source values remain null and these polygons remain visible but unranked. Income is joined by official numeric neighbourhood code, not by name.

This measure adjusts for household size/composition but is based on tax statistics. It excludes tax-exempt income such as child allowances and social integration income, and can omit or underestimate international-employment and capital/property income. It is not administrative disposable income and is not a poverty rate.

Amsterdam O&S IHHINK_MED is non-equivalised disposable household income; Brussels indicator 2498 is equivalised after-tax income of residents. These are explicitly different income definitions. Their use is limited to separate within-city sensitivity experiments. No comparison of euro levels, poverty rates, city performance or equivalent vulnerability between cities is supported. Percentile normalization does not remove differences in definitions.

Brussels temperature is extracted from Landsat with a WorldCover non-water mask. Its green indicator is calculated by vector intersection as described above. No 500 m grid scores enter either city’s neighbourhood model. Both cities retain the same four weight sets.

Limitations

Input dates, household adjustments, income coverage and local geographies differ. Neighbourhood medians hide within-area inequalities. Some groups and sources of income are not fully represented. No predictive validation or new spatial-scale sensitivity metric is claimed.
