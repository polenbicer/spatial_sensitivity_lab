Methods & reproducibility

Release status: v0.3. IBSA and CBS social indicators are integrated where both measures are available. The vulnerability-informed ranking excludes cells without sufficient social data.

Research scope

Who Is Prioritized? The Politics of Data-Driven Urban Cooling and Territorial Governance in Brussels and Amsterdam. This prototype is a critical research instrument: it tests value sensitivity in rankings. Policy and decision documents and planned expert interviews examine the institutional stages from defining needs through interpreting evidence to setting intervention priorities. It does not represent an existing municipal decision system. Procurement and budget decisions are possible later stages, not systematically traced in this study.

Research question: How are contested definitions of urban cooling need translated into spatial and institutional priorities in Brussels and Amsterdam, and under what conditions can this decision-making process be considered democratically legitimate?

  1. Value sensitivity: which rankings change under different available indicators and weights?
  2. Decision chain: who fixes contestable choices, at which stages, and with what justification?
  3. Authority and contestation: whose knowledge counts, and how can affected groups participate in or challenge priorities?

Data versions and boundaries

  • Amsterdam: PDOK administrative boundary; CBS neighbourhood layer labelled 2025 in the export script.
  • Brussels: UrbIS regional boundary and the 145 IBSA / perspective.brussels monitoring neighbourhood polygons, downloaded 29 September 2026 (portal data processed 29 September 2026).
  • ESA WorldCover 2021 (10 m); Copernicus imperviousness 2021 (10 m); GHSL GHS-POP 2020 (100 m).
  • Landsat 8/9 Collection 2 Level 2: June–August 2019–2023.

The social age and household indicators are 2025 for both cities, but Brussels median taxable income is 2023 and is diagnostic metadata only. Physical and population layers use 2019–2023, 2020 and 2021 sources. Scores compare cells within each city and are not direct city-to-city outcome comparisons.

Processing and code

  1. Project source rasters and boundaries to a common analytical CRS.
  2. Intersect with a 500 m grid, calculate land cover and population indicators, then normalise within each city.
  3. Apply four disclosed weight combinations and their median; aggregate neighbourhood summaries by cell overlap area.
  4. Validate association with Landsat surface temperature using Random Forest and five spatial folds.

The public code includes the social join, spatial overlay, within-city percentile transformation, scenario scoring, rank correlations and web export. Run python3 scripts/build_social_diagnostic.py after installing Shapely, pyproj and SciPy. The IBSA source tables are archived in the repository; the boundary export is retrieved from its cited portal at build time. The upstream raster processing GeoPackage, original model training code and random seed are not deposited, so thermal model reproduction remains partial. The Git commit is an immutable code reference; a release tag is pending.

Limitations and planned checks

  • MAUP: grid size and neighbourhood boundaries can change rankings.
  • GHSL 2020 population is modelled, not observed per cell.
  • Indicators span 2019–2025, not one year.
  • Social diagnostic coverage: 624 of 732 eligible Brussels cells, 886 of 949 Amsterdam cells. IBSA marks 27 neighbourhoods VS/ND, mainly parks, cemeteries and industrial areas. No values were imputed. Mixed-neighbourhood cells can combine different social profiles and risk ecological inference.
  • Area weighting assumes even distribution within each neighbourhood. A population-weighted overlay requires a finer population raster aligned to the polygons; the existing cell-level GHSL density would not provide a valid independent within-cell comparison.
  • No uncertainty intervals for priority scores.
  • Balanced versus vulnerability-informed, on complete cells: Brussels Spearman 0.9541, Kendall 0.8130, top-20% overlap 80.8%; Amsterdam 0.9792, 0.8784 and 89.9%. Further spatial unit and uncertainty tests remain planned.

Download schema

Social diagnostic JSON is an array of rows: [grid ID, age 65+ %, one-person households %, Brussels median taxable income €, social diagnostic 0–100, vulnerability-informed priority 0–100, age coverage %, household coverage %, intersecting neighbourhood count]. Income is null for Amsterdam and is not in the scenario. Nulls are retained for missing values. Join on grid ID to the environmental GeoJSON.
