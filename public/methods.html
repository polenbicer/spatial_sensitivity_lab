Methods & reproducibility

Amsterdam income model: 40% surface pressure, 30% green deficit, 30% low income. Four earlier population-based scenarios remain available. Brussels has no income model yet.

Research scope

Who Is Prioritized? The Politics of Data-Driven Urban Cooling and Territorial Governance in Brussels and Amsterdam. This prototype is a critical research instrument: it tests value sensitivity in rankings. Technical performance alone cannot justify allocating public attention and resources. The study develops and assesses conditions of explanation, affected people’s input, contestation, revision and clear institutional responsibility. Its findings will inform a concise checklist for public authorities. Policy and decision documents and planned expert interviews examine the institutional stages from defining needs through interpreting evidence to setting intervention priorities. It does not represent an existing municipal decision system. Procurement and budget decisions are possible later stages, not systematically traced in this study.

Research question: How do data-driven approaches translate contested understandings of urban cooling need into spatial and institutional priorities, and under what conditions can this process be considered democratically legitimate? The question is examined through Brussels and Amsterdam.

  1. Value sensitivity: how do indicator selection and weighting change rankings in Brussels and Amsterdam, and which areas are most affected?
  2. Decision chain: how are cooling needs and priorities defined, interpreted and justified in institutions, and when do contestable choices become difficult to revise?
  3. Democratic legitimacy: whose knowledge influences choices, what opportunities exist to question or contest them, and what conditions of justification, participation and responsibility should govern their use?

Income: source and transfer to the grid

Source: CBS median standardised disposable household income for 2023, on 2025 neighbourhood boundaries. 420 of 517 source neighbourhoods have a published value; 97 do not. Two additional water areas have no matching source row. Source values in thousands of euros are converted to euros.

Transfer: intersect the site’s clipped 500 m cells with CBS 2025 neighbourhood polygons in EPSG:3035. Calculate an overlap-area-weighted mean of the published neighbourhood medians. This is a neighbourhood-based proxy, not the median income of households in a cell. Household counts and distributions inside neighbourhoods are not inferred. The site’s 519 boundaries match official CBS/PDOK 2025 codes; same-code geometry overlap is at least 99.5%.

Missing data: a cell requires at least 99.9% of its clipped area to have published income before it receives a proxy or score. The 0.1% tolerance addresses geometry differences; it is an analyst’s completeness rule, not a CBS standard. Partial and absent coverage remain unscored. No missing income is set to zero, and available pieces are not silently treated as the whole cell.

Coverage: 365 of 949 eligible Amsterdam cells receive an income score; 584 do not. The full overlay also includes 100 ineligible cells, which do not enter rankings. Coverage refers to area, not the share of households. The selection may exclude particular local conditions, so conclusions apply to the covered cells only.

Scoring: rank the income proxies inversely among the 365 eligible complete-coverage cells, using average ranks for ties and scaling to 0–100. Lower income gives a higher score. The income model combines 40% surface pressure + 30% green deficit + 30% low income; population is not in this model. These weights are an explicit analytical choice. Neither income score nor priority is a poverty rate.

Neighbourhood summaries: aggregate eligible grid scores by exact overlap area. Publish an income scenario summary only when at least 99.9% of eligible overlap area has a score. 125 neighbourhoods have such a summary; ordinary mapped-coverage eligibility still applies. This aggregation is distinct from the original CBS neighbourhood median.

Comparison: rank movements are recalculated using only locations with scores in both selected scenarios. A change in the number of observations must not be mistaken for an effect of weighting. The four-scenario median and Priority Lines continue to summarise the original population-based scenarios and exclude the income model.

Brussels: no equivalent neighbourhood disposable-income dataset is integrated. Brussels income scores are unavailable; there is no cross-city income ranking.

Official sources: CBS time series, original XLSX, CBS 2025 boundaries, PDOK services, Statbel disposable income. The JSON overlay records source URLs, hashes, coverage, weights and statuses. Download the grid CSV. The transfer script reproduces this step; income preparation uses the archived source workbook.

Processing and code

  1. Project source rasters and boundaries to a common analytical CRS.
  2. Intersect with a 500 m grid, calculate land cover and population indicators, then normalise within each city.
  3. Apply four disclosed weight combinations and their median; aggregate neighbourhood summaries by cell overlap area.
  4. Validate association with Landsat surface temperature using Random Forest and five spatial folds.

Processing scripts are available in the source repository. The original thermal model training workflow is not fully archived, so its reproduction remains partial.

Limitations and planned checks

  • MAUP: grid size and neighbourhood boundaries can change rankings.
  • GHSL 2020 population is modelled, not observed per cell.
  • Inputs span several years: 2019–2023 summer imagery, 2020 population, 2021 land cover, 2023 income and 2025 boundaries.
  • Area weighting assumes even distribution within each neighbourhood. A population-weighted overlay requires a finer population raster aligned to the polygons; the existing cell-level GHSL density would not provide a valid independent within-cell comparison.
  • No uncertainty intervals for priority scores.

Download schema

Grid GeoJSON contains the original environmental and population scenarios. Join amsterdam_income.json grid_rows by grid_id and neighbourhood_rows by id to add the income model. Missing scores remain null. income_proxy_eur is an area-weighted proxy of neighbourhood medians. income_coverage_pct is area coverage; priority_income uses the disclosed 40/30/30 formula. Both JSON and grid CSV are downloadable above.
