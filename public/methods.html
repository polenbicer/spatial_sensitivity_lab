Methods & sources

Every scenario uses the same three indicators: surface temperature, green deficit and low income. Only weights change. No separate income scenario remains.

The scenario median is the median of these four scores. It is a statistical summary, not public agreement. The weights are analytical choices.

Surface temperature

Observed median summer land-surface temperature from Landsat 8/9 Collection 2 Level 2, June–August 2019–2023. Each city's eligible cells with observed temperature receive an average-rank percentile scaled to 0–100. Higher temperature gives a higher score. Reference counts: Amsterdam 843, Brussels 732. This is surface temperature, not air temperature or a sealed-surface proxy. Missing temperature is not estimated from another indicator.

Landsat source and product definition.

Green deficit

The existing 0–100 within-city deficit score is retained, using cooling-green classes from ESA WorldCover 2021 (10 m). Higher deficit gives a higher priority signal. The original raster-to-grid processing is not fully reproducible from this repository alone; the revised scenario calculations are reproducible from the published grid.

ESA WorldCover 2021.

Low income

CBS median standardised disposable household income for 2023, on 2025 neighbourhood boundaries. Of 517 source neighbourhoods, 420 have a published value and 97 do not. Two additional water areas have no source row. Raw units of 1,000 EUR are converted to EUR.

Exact polygon intersections in EPSG:3035 transfer an area-weighted mean of neighbourhood medians to the clipped 500 m grid. This is a neighbourhood-based proxy, not a measured cell household median. At least 99.9% of cell area must have published income. The 0.1% tolerance is an analyst’s geometry tolerance, not a CBS rule. Incomplete coverage is not extrapolated and missing values are not replaced by zero.

The inverse average-rank percentile uses the 365 eligible complete-income cells as its reference. Lower income gives a higher score; ties receive equal scores. This is not a poverty rate. CBS/PDOK 2025 codes match all 519 site areas; same-code geometry overlap is at least 99.5%.

Coverage and ranking

Every weighting requires all three indicators. Amsterdam has 307 complete eligible cells out of 949: 584 lack complete income coverage and another 58 have income but no observed surface temperature. All four weightings use the same 307 cells. Grey areas are unranked, not low priority.

Brussels has no ranking while comparable neighbourhood income remains unavailable. Its temperature and green indicators remain inspectable. No previous ranking is used as a substitute.

Neighbourhood summaries use exact overlap-area-weighted means of complete cell scores. A neighbourhood needs at least 99.9% coverage of eligible overlap area and 50% mapped neighbourhood coverage to enter rankings. Rank changes compare only common, complete observations. Priority Lines uses the same four scenarios and the same neighbourhood scores as Rankings.

Limits

The mapped income proxy does not describe household variation within a cell. Area coverage is not household coverage. Input years differ. Relative scores are not cross-city performance measures. No new machine-learning validation, uncertainty interval or 500 m/1 km sensitivity result is claimed for this revised index. Earlier model metrics do not validate the current three-indicator model.

Downloads and reproduction

Grid GeoJSON · Neighbourhood GeoJSON · Amsterdam grid CSV · Income, current model scores and provenance. All scenario scores are already in the GeoJSON files; no manual join is needed.

Run python3 scripts/rebuild_priority_models.py, then node scripts/validate-data.mjs. The script retains income transfer and green deficit, recalculates heat percentiles, all four priorities and neighbourhood summaries. The validation checks unchanged weights, score direction, identical complete-cell coverage and missing values. Source code.
